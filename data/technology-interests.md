# What I enjoy about technology

## Computers and hardware

I've liked computers since school. Learning what the operating system does and what parts like the GPU are for made me want to understand the whole machine. Writing an HTML page in seventh grade was another turning point. I could write a few lines and see the result in a browser. That immediate feedback is still something I enjoy about coding and app development.

I follow new graphics cards and chips designed for AI. I also pay attention when new laptops or phones come out. I like experimenting with what I can already run on my own laptop, whether that's a game or something I'm trying out with AI. If I had a free afternoon with a robotic arm, I'd pick an ordinary task and see if I could automate it. Getting a real device to do something is what makes the idea interesting to me.

## Cyber forensics and AI

My favorite courses this semester are Cyber Forensics and Agentic AI. In forensics, the part I find most interesting is reconstructing what happened while keeping the original evidence intact. The first time I saw traces of old files on a USB drive, I realized how different deleting a file can be from removing every trace of it. That experience helped me understand why preserving evidence matters.

Agentic AI appeals to me because I like building things. I'd already studied deep learning, but I had been putting off building an agent. Seeing the tools in class made it feel like something I could try sooner. I use ChatGPT when I'm stuck and need a direction to explore. I still want to be able to read the code, understand the fundamentals, and debug it myself. Those are skills I'd tell a new student to practice early.

If I had to choose between AI and cybersecurity as a career right now, I'd lean toward cybersecurity. I still want to get better at app development and AI, and I like that computer science gives me room to work on all three. My immediate goal is to finish practical projects that I can explain from experience and add them to my portfolio.
