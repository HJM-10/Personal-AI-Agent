# What I'd tell a new computer science student at UMT Sialkot

## Treat the semester as a series of assessments

When I came to university from O- and A-levels, I was used to looking ahead to a final exam. I took that same attitude into my first semesters. A quiz or another assessment looked small enough to put off, so I didn't always give it the attention I should have. Seeing the effect on my GPA changed how I thought about the semester. My advice is to find out how your course is assessed early and keep up with the smaller assessments while you're learning the material.

The first two weeks also took some adjusting. We had timetable clashes and a classroom that didn't fit our section. I had a Programming Fundamentals instructor I really liked, and the instructor changed early on. I remember being disappointed. Looking back, that was part of learning how university worked and finding a way to stay engaged when the semester started differently from what I'd expected.

## Get comfortable finding your own bugs

If I could give someone in their first semester one piece of advice, it would be to spend time reading and debugging their own code. Get comfortable with programming fundamentals and object-oriented programming. When an LLM gives you an answer that looks right, try to explain what the program does. Knowing how to follow the code makes it easier to make changes later and to notice when an answer is taking you in the wrong direction.

Take theory courses seriously too. They count toward your GPA, and they give you a chance to think through analytical problems. I enjoy a hard assignment more once I understand what the question is asking and can see how it connects to something we've covered.

## Build something you can explain

I wish I'd paid attention to my portfolio earlier. Learning syntax, libraries, and frameworks gave me a foundation, and working on projects taught me how those things fit together. Spend Trial began with a problem I could explain easily. I wanted a better way to see where my own spending went. A project like that gives you a reason to make decisions and something specific to talk about when someone asks what you built.

When I start a project, I try to understand what the user needs and talk through my idea with them. Then I write down what we agreed on, choose the tools and database, and sketch the schema. I start with the basics and work toward the more complicated parts. I like being able to go back to those notes when I get stuck.
